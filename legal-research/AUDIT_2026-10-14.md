# Document Builder — full audit (44 documents, English + Arabic)

Date: 2026-10-14. Method: (1) automated check of every document in both languages and every switch state (`scripts/docAudit/mech.ts`); (2) five independent reviewers, each assigned about nine documents, who read the rendered text and the template source, checked Maryland and federal rules against primary sources where they could, and proposed exact fixes; (3) all HIGH and MEDIUM findings were implemented, then re-tested (264 PDF/Word exports, 0 failures). This is research and drafting work, **not legal advice**. Several rules below were taken from summaries of the statute pages and must be confirmed against the official text by a Maryland attorney.

## Cross-cutting fixes made
- Final pay, pay frequency, paydays, overtime and leave notice (Labor & Employment §3-502, 3-504, 3-505); employee-warning/termination letters protect at-will status; whistleblower notice (18 U.S.C. §1833(b)) in confidentiality clauses.
- Maryland deposit cap for residential leases is **one month's rent** (RP §8-203); late fee cap 5% (RP §8-208); lead-paint, Tenants' Bill of Rights, disclosure/disclaimer receipts added.
- Bulk-transfer notice and Comptroller holdback replace the old "tax clearance" wording in sale-of-business documents (Commercial Law Title 6; Tax-General §11-505, 13-802).
- Official forms are stated as controlling where required: Comptroller **Form 548 only** (not IRS 2848/8821), Maryland unemployment-insurance has its own POA form.
- Records must be returned on request even if a fee is unpaid (31 CFR §10.28) — fee agreement, engagement letter, termination letter, payment plan.
- Signature blocks: firm/employer signature lines on letters, printed name and title for companies ("By / Name / Title"), notary acknowledgments instead of "sworn" lines on powers of attorney, child-travel consent reworked.
- Engine bug fixed: a number or amount of 0 used to count as "filled in" (printed "0 days", "$0.00", "0%").
- Arabic: number–noun agreement, deposit vs insurance terms, "default" terminology, notary-commission translation, gender-neutral phrasing.
