# Client Privacy Notice (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act) — AL TAX SERVICE

Drafted 2026-10-15 for the owner. Not yet delivered to anyone. Read "How to use this" first; the notice itself starts at the line "FACTS".

## How to use this (read first)

**Which rule applies.** The brief named 16 CFR Part 313. That is no longer the right rule for a tax preparer. 16 CFR 313.1(b) now covers only motor-vehicle dealers ("persons described in 12 U.S.C. 5519 that are predominantly engaged in the sale and servicing of motor vehicles"). The privacy-notice rule for a tax preparer is the CFPB's Regulation P, **12 CFR Part 1016**. The FTC's **Safeguards Rule, 16 CFR Part 314**, still applies to tax preparers (see WISP.md). A tax preparer is a "financial institution": 16 CFR 314.2(h)(2)(viii) — "An accountant or other tax preparation service that is in the business of completing income tax returns is a financial institution".

**Who must get the notice.** 12 CFR 1016.1(b)(1): the rule covers only individuals who obtain a financial product or service "primarily for personal, family, or household purposes", and "does not apply to information about companies or about individuals who obtain financial products or services for business, commercial, or agricultural purposes." So:
- Individuals whose personal Form 1040 (or Maryland 502/505) you prepare are **customers**. 12 CFR 1016.4(c)(3)(i)(D): a customer relationship is established when the consumer "Becomes your client for the purpose of your providing credit counseling or tax preparation services." The initial notice is due "not later than when you establish a customer relationship" (1016.4(a)(1)).
- Corporations, LLCs and other companies are not covered by this rule. An owner who is also a 1040 client is covered for the 1040 work. Simplest safe practice: give this notice to **every individual** who becomes a client, including business owners. It costs nothing and cannot be wrong.
- Employees of business clients (payroll) are not your customers; their data is protected by the WISP, not by this notice.

**How to deliver it (1016.9).** Written notice, so that the client "can reasonably be expected to receive actual notice in writing or, if the consumer agrees, electronically." Acceptable: hand a printed copy, mail it, or (for a client who transacts electronically) post it in the portal and require the client to acknowledge receipt before continuing (1016.9(b)(1)(iii)(A)). **Not acceptable:** only a sign in the office, or only explaining it out loud (1016.9(d): "You may not provide any notice required by this part solely by orally explaining the notice"). A privacy-policy page on the website alone does not satisfy the rule. Keep the signed acknowledgment (or the portal acknowledgment record) in the client file.

**Annual notice.** Not required if you (1) share non-public personal information with outside companies only for the reasons in 1016.13, 1016.14 and 1016.15 (service providers, doing what the client asked, legal and regulatory reasons) and (2) have not changed those policies since the last notice (12 CFR 1016.5(e)(1)). This notice is written so that both conditions hold. If the firm ever starts sharing for any other reason, a new notice with an opt-out is needed first. A former client stops being a customer when you "have provided and received payment for the service and no longer provide any statements or notices to the customer concerning that relationship" (1016.5(b)(3)(v) describes this for FTC-jurisdiction entities; apply it).

**Model form.** The wording below follows the CFPB/FTC model privacy form (appendix to 12 CFR Part 1016). Using the model form exactly gives a safe harbor; the official layout is a two-page, boxed table, so for the printed copy keep the section titles and the table rows in this order and do not add sections. The customizable definitions below ("has no affiliates", "does not share with nonaffiliates so they can market to you", "doesn't jointly market") are the exact wording the appendix prescribes. I could not retrieve the form's graphic layout from the eCFR text, so before printing, compare it once with the sample at consumerfinance.gov ("Model privacy form"). That is the only item I could not check against primary text.

**Facts this notice relies on (all verified in the code or in the firm's public statements; change the notice if any becomes untrue):**
- The firm sells nothing and gives no client information to outside companies for their own marketing.
- The firm has no affiliates. **[CONFIRM]** — if the owner owns another company that receives client data, change the "Affiliates" definition.
- Outside providers receive client data only to run the service: hosting/database (Railway, Neon), email (Resend), text and WhatsApp (Twilio), and tax agencies / e-file / payroll platforms when the firm files for the client.

## Notes for staff (not part of the notice)

1. **Arabic.** Many clients read Arabic better than English. The regulation does not require a translation, but "clear and conspicuous" (1016.3(b)) means the client must be able to understand it. Give an Arabic copy to a client who asks. Have it translated by a qualified person; the privacy page on the website (privacy.html) already carries an Arabic edition in the same plain style.
2. **Related Maryland rule.** A registered individual tax preparer "may not disclose information received for the purposes of preparing a customer's federal or State income tax return, unless: (i) consented to in writing by the customer; (ii) expressly authorized by law; (iii) necessary for the preparation of the tax return; or (iv) in accordance with a court order." (Md. Code, Bus. Occ. & Prof. §21-403(c)(3).) The notice above is consistent with that; do not share return information with anyone (including a lender or a relative) without written consent.
3. **IRC §7216.** Using or disclosing tax return information for anything other than preparing the return (for example, offering the client a bank product, or sending the information to a marketing vendor) needs the client's signed consent that meets Treas. Reg. §301.7216-3. The firm does none of this today; do not start without a consent form.
4. **When to issue a new notice.** If the firm begins sharing client information with an outside company for any purpose other than the three allowed reasons, STOP and issue a revised notice with an opt-out before sharing (12 CFR 1016.8).
5. **Marketing to clients.** The "For our marketing purposes — Yes / No" row only says the firm itself may use client information to tell clients about its own services (reminders, newsletter). Every marketing email has an unsubscribe link; text messages follow sms-terms.html.
