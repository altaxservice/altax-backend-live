## Follow-up research (2026-10-15)

**Individual tax preparers (COMAR 09.38, retrieved from Cornell LII's copy of COMAR; the dsd.maryland.gov page was not reachable, so confirm against the official text before relying on a number).**
- Registration (BOP §21-302, §21-304; COMAR 09.38.01.02): age 18, high-school diploma or equivalent, good character, pass the Board's examination (passing score 70 percent). An IRS Registered Tax Return Preparer exam passed 11/1/2011 to 1/18/2013 counts. A waiver of the exam needs 15 consecutive years of experience (average 50 returns a year, never fewer than 25), 8 hours of annual continuing education and good standing. The Board's page requires a current IRS PTIN each tax year; the IRS Annual Filing Season Program is not a substitute for the exam.
- Fees (COMAR 09.38.01.04): registration $100, renewal $100, reinstatement $120. Registration lasts two years (§21-308); renewal needs 16 hours of continuing education, 4 of them on Maryland tax (§21-309; COMAR 09.38.02.02); keep course records 4 years (09.38.02.03G).
- Disclosure (§21-403(b)): no regulation found that adds to the five statutory items. The Board's own manual says the Board "has authority to impose other requirements as well," and I found none. Item (iv) is "education and training, including examinations taken and successfully passed": name each exam actually taken and passed; describe a waiver or an IRS program as what it is, not as an exam passed. Give it at the initial meeting and, if the preparer has a website, on the website.
- Record retention (§21-403(a)): the Board's period may not be longer than federal law; no Maryland regulation sets one. Federal: a signing preparer keeps a copy or a list of returns for 3 years after the return period (26 CFR §1.6107-1(b)(2)).
- Conduct rules that affect the contracts (COMAR 09.38.01.05): no contingent fees (C); no refund check payable to the preparer (A(5)); give a client a copy of the return and personal papers on written request within a reasonable time, a fee allowed (B); no advertising that implies unsupported credentials or states a fee without disclosing the variables (G(3)(d), (e)); tell a client of any conflict of interest (D).
- Exemptions (§21-102(b)): active CPAs, attorneys in good standing, IRS-enrolled agents, government employees on duty, and employees or assistants of a registered preparer, except one who signs a return as preparer (§21-102(c)).
- Changes made: personal tax preparation letter (English and Arabic) now says the disclosure includes exams taken and passed, is given at the first meeting before work starts, and that the fee does not depend on the refund or the result; `tax_preparer_disclosure` readiness notes extended.

**Jury waiver and shortened claims period.**
- Walther v. Sovereign Bank, 386 Md. 412 (2005): "Although the right to a jury trial is fundamental, parties can contractually waive their right to a jury trial." There "ordinarily must exist a 'knowing and intelligent' waiver." The court enforced a waiver in a consumer mortgage arbitration clause that was underlined and sat right above the signatures. Maryland did not exempt consumers; conspicuousness is the main test. The court cited cases refusing waivers buried in small print. The case is about a waiver inside an arbitration clause; I found no Maryland appellate opinion on a stand-alone waiver in a service contract.
- Rule 2-511(a) preserves the jury right "inviolate"; Rule 2-325(b) treats a late jury demand as a waiver. Neither forbids a contract waiver.
- Bank of America v. Mitchell Living Trust, 822 F. Supp. 2d 505 (D. Md. 2011): in federal court the question is federal law; the party enforcing must show a knowing and voluntary waiver, judged by relative bargaining power, conspicuousness and comprehensibility.
- Ceccone v. Carroll Home Services, 454 Md. 680 (2017): a shortened limitations period is valid only if no statute says otherwise, no fraud or duress, and it is "reasonable in light of all the circumstances" (length, bargaining power, subject matter, one-sidedness). CJ §5-101 is the 3-year default.
- Clause 14 (Business Clients only, capital letters, own paragraph, now with a plain-language sentence, Arabic edition) and clause 15 (mutual two years, Business Clients only) fit these tests. Individual Clients keep their jury right, the safest reading given the consumer cases. Meyer v. State Farm, 85 Md. App. 83 (1990) is the intermediate case Walther quotes (arbitration implies waiver); the earlier "385 Md. 175" citation was wrong.
